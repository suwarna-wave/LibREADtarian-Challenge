# Liberty Data Drive 2026

## What we are doing

This project is our working space for the Liberty Data Drive competition.

Our goal is to study a real policy change that happened in multiple countries, examine what happened to measurable human-development outcomes afterward, and use the evidence to recommend exactly one specific policy change for a South Asian country.

The final result should feel like the work of a very good senior data analyst:

- focused,
- evidence-driven,
- reproducible,
- visually clear,
- and easy to defend.

We are **not** trying to build the most complicated model.

We are trying to build the strongest argument the data honestly supports.

# Datasets

We use two fixed datasets.

## 1. Fraser Institute — Economic Freedom of the World

This is the **policy / institutional** side.

We will focus on specific sub-components instead of only the total economic-freedom score.

Possible areas include:

- trade restrictions,
- legal system and property rights,
- regulation,
- sound money,
- related sub-components.

## 2. UNDP Human Development data

This is the **outcome** side.

Likely outcome variables:

- GNI per capita,
- life expectancy,
- expected years of schooling,
- mean years of schooling.

We should generally prefer these real-world measures over the overall HDI score.

## Step 3 — Find strong candidate policy changes

Explore the EFW sub-components and look for countries with clear, meaningful changes.

We want policy areas where:

- at least 3 countries show comparable change,
- the change can be connected to a real policy reform,
- the reform year can be verified,
- the UNDP outcome data covers enough years before and after,
- the policy has a meaningful South Asian equivalent.

We should explore several ideas before choosing one.

# Candidate directions

These are starting areas, not predetermined answers.

## Trade liberalization

Possible EFW measure:
- freedom to trade / tariff-related sub-components

Possible outcome:
- GNI per capita

Strength:
- easy to understand,
- often clear reform dates,
- many historical cases,
- direct South Asian relevance.

Risk:
- trade reforms often happen alongside wider economic reforms.

---

## Legal system / property rights

Possible outcome:
- GNI per capita

Strength:
- strong liberty argument,

Risk:

---

## Regulation

Possible outcome:
- GNI per capita

Strength:
- possible clear policy recommendation,
- direct connection to barriers to entry or business activity.

Risk:
- the regulation category can be too broad unless we find a specific sub-component.

---

## Sound money / monetary institutions

Possible outcome:
- GNI per capita or longer-run development outcomes.

Strength:
- strong mechanism around inflation and economic stability.

Risk:
- monetary stabilization usually happens during broader economic crises or reforms.

# How we choose the final topic

A candidate should score well on these questions:

1. Is the EFW variable a good measure of the policy?
2. Can we find at least 3 good country cases?
3. Can we identify the real reform dates?
4. Is there enough UNDP data before and after?
5. Is the policy-to-outcome mechanism believable?
6. Can we explain alternative explanations honestly?
7. Is there a specific South Asian policy we could recommend?
8. Is there a clear liberty argument?
9. Can the story fit comfortably into 10 slides?
10. Can every result be reproduced easily?

The strongest topic is the one that performs well across all ten, not simply the one with the biggest-looking graph.

---

# Research question

A good question should look like:

> Among countries that made [specific policy change], was the reform followed by [specific measurable outcome] during the following [X] years, and what does this suggest for [specific policy] in [South Asian country]?

It should clearly identify:

- policy,
- countries,
- time period,
- outcome.

---

# Country selection

We should avoid choosing countries after seeing their outcomes.

Instead, define a rule where possible.

Example:

> Countries where the selected EFW trade indicator improved by more than X points during a defined period.

Then verify whether each numerical change corresponds to a real policy reform.

If four countries support the hypothesis and one does not, we should keep the fifth country.

That makes the project more credible.

---

# Reform-year analysis

Countries will probably reform at different times.

Example:

```text
Country A — 1991
Country B — 1994
Country C — 1998
Country D — 2002
```

We can align them using:

```python
event_time = year - reform_year
```

Then compare:

```text
-5 = five years before reform
 0 = reform year
+5 = five years after reform
+10 = ten years after reform
```

This is likely to be one of the most useful analytical views in the project.

# Analysis philosophy

Start simple.

Useful tools include:

- time-series charts,
- before/after comparisons,
- percentage change,
- average annual growth,
- event-time charts,
- small multiples.

We do not automatically need:

- machine learning,
- complicated regressions,
- causal inference frameworks,
- dashboards,
- advanced statistical tests.

Only use them if they clearly improve the answer.

---

# Causal honesty

We should not say:

> Trade reform caused GNI to rise.

We can say:

> GNI growth accelerated after the reform in four of five cases.

or:

> The pattern is consistent with the reform contributing to stronger income growth.

Then immediately consider other explanations.

This makes the analysis stronger, not weaker.

---

# Policy research

For every country case, collect:

- what policy changed,
- when it took effect,
- who enacted it,
- the relevant law/regulation/programme,
- a public URL,
- date accessed.

Use free public sources only.

Prefer official sources whenever possible.

Keep all of this in:

```text
research/policy_notes.md
```

---

# Final recommendation

We must make exactly one recommendation.

It must be one of:

- Addition
- Removal
- Modification

And it must identify:

1. the exact policy,
2. the responsible authority,
3. how the change is supposed to work,
4. which of our charts support it,
5. the strongest argument against it.

The recommendation must target:

- Afghanistan
- Bangladesh
- Bhutan
- India
- Maldives
- Nepal
- Pakistan
- Sri Lanka.

A precise recommendation is better than several vague recommendations.

---

# Liberty reasoning

The submission should also explain why the reform matters from a liberty perspective.

Possible questions:

- Does the reform expand voluntary choice?
- Does it reduce arbitrary government discretion?
- Does it lower barriers to entry?
- Does it strengthen contract or property rights?
- Does it reduce privilege for one group at the expense of another?
- Who bears the cost?
- Are there legitimate harms or tradeoffs?

The liberty argument should be principled, not merely ideological.
