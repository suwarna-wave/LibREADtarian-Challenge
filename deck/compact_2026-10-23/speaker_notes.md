## Slide 10 — Wider choice—with the costs stated openly

**Point to:** Give the objection equal seriousness. Pause before the closing line; do not read the four appendices aloud.

**Say:**

The liberty argument is that people should have wider freedom to exchange under predictable rules. Tariff protection can concentrate benefits among selected producers while distributing costs across many buyers, including businesses using imported inputs. But removing protection also has real costs. Government revenue may fall, and domestic firms and workers can face concentrated losses. Those concerns matter both for livelihoods and for public services. A gradual transition changes the timing of adjustment; it does not make the costs disappear. My judgement is to phase down remaining high basic duties, while being clear that this study does not establish an optimal cap, a revenue gain or a guaranteed growth benefit. Income usually increased after reform, but the growth comparisons were mixed. That is the conclusion the evidence supports. Let evidence guide policy, and let people choose. Thank you. I welcome your questions. The four appendix pages provide the index, public references and full disclosure for verification.
